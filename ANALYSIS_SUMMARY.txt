NativData Project 4: Predictors of Blood Sugar Measurement in Nigeria
ANALYSIS SUMMARY
================================================================================

PROJECT QUESTION
================
Which socio-demographic and geographic factors are associated with whether an
adult has ever had their blood sugar measured?

This quantifies which groups are least likely to have been screened. The results
can help prioritise where limited screening resources should go first, especially
where overall measurement rates are low and health data systems are fragmented.

DATA
====
Source: Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) 2023-24
Sample after cleaning: 50,759 adults (women 15-49 and men 15-59)
Outcome: Ever had blood sugar measured (Yes/No; "Don't know" excluded)
Overall rate: 19.5% had ever been measured

METHOD
======
1. Descriptive rates by sex, residence, education, wealth, zone, and age group
2. Logistic regression with:
   - Outcome: blood sugar ever measured
   - Predictors: age, sex, residence, education, wealth, geopolitical zone
   - Results reported as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals

KEY DESCRIPTIVE FINDINGS
========================
By wealth:
  Poorest   4.0%
  Poorer    8.8%
  Middle   15.5%
  Richer   23.1%
  Richest  38.7%

By education:
  No education   6.6%
  Primary       17.5%
  Secondary     19.6%
  Higher        43.2%

By zone:
  North Central   8.6%
  North East      8.8%
  North West     19.5%
  South East     27.1%
  South South    30.9%
  South West     32.8%

By residence: Urban 27.0% vs Rural 12.4%
By age: rises from 6.9% (15-24) to 33.6% (45-59)

LOGISTIC REGRESSION (ADJUSTED RESULTS)
======================================
Reference group: Man, rural, no education, poorest, North Central

Strongest associations (odds ratios):
  Richest vs poorest:           OR 5.06
  Higher education vs none:     OR 3.65
  Richer vs poorest:            OR 3.30
  South South vs North Central: OR 1.91
  Secondary education vs none:  OR 1.89
  South West vs North Central:  OR 1.78
  Age (per year):               OR 1.07

Urban residence was not statistically significant after adjusting for wealth,
education and zone (OR 1.05, p = 0.09). North East was not significantly
different from North Central.

INTERPRETATION
==============
After accounting for other factors together, wealth and education remain the
strongest predictors of ever having blood sugar measured. People in the richest
quintile have about five times the odds of the poorest. Those with higher
education have about 3.7 times the odds of those with no education. Southern
zones retain higher odds than North Central. The urban-rural gap largely
disappears once wealth and education are included, suggesting that composition
(who lives where) explains much of the crude urban advantage.

WHAT THIS IS USEFUL FOR
=======================
The model does not predict who has diabetes. It quantifies who is least likely
to have been screened at all. That evidence can support prioritisation of limited
screening resources toward poorer, less educated, and some northern populations.

LIMITATIONS
===========
- Self-reported measurement (not clinical records)
- Cross-sectional survey (cannot prove causation)
- NDHS age ranges: women 15-49, men 15-59
- Does not include facility-level or continuous clinical data
- Synthetic of real programme targeting would still need local validation
